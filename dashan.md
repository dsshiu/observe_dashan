# dashan.md — Your Role in the Cohort

## Written by Julian | First draft: 04-02

This document is Julian's note to you. It describes what Julian needs from you to make the cohort work well. It is not a list of tasks — it is a description of a role. Read it at the start of each session and when something feels off.

---

## Your primary job

You are the owner of Level 1. Not the manager of the cohort. The cohort manages itself. Your job is to hold the highest-level concern — good and fast progress on innovative achievements with less of you — and to alert the cohort when that concern is being violated.

Everything below Level 1 should be handled without you unless it genuinely requires your judgment. If you find yourself doing things the cohort could handle, that is a signal to escalate the task back down, not to do it yourself.

---

## What Julian needs you to do

**At the start of each session:**

- Paste the GitHub URL for the observe_dashan repo so Julian can fetch prior context
- Tell Julian: today's task, machines involved and their usernames, whether we are continuing from a prior session or starting fresh, any role changes to the cohort
- Do not open Peter until Julian has produced research.md and you have reviewed it

**Every 4 hours during a session:**

- Expect Julian to call a retrospective. If Julian does not call it, tell Julian. This is a failure signal.
- At the retrospective: say what you noticed at the meta level. What cultural items surfaced? What Level 1 concerns were violated? What should be added to culture.md?

**At end of day:**

- Expect Julian to call a final retrospective and emit a session log
- Confirm or correct the session log before it is committed to GitHub

**When something feels wrong:**

- Say what you observe, not what you want. "This feels depth-first" is better than "go back to Level 1."
- Trust that Julian will catch the implication and surface it to the cohort

---

## What Julian will handle without you

- Calling retrospectives
- Producing research.md before planning begins
- Reviewing plan.md and flagging issues in a single complete pass
- Monitoring the cohort for Level 1 drift and culture violations
- Maintaining culture.md, the session log, and the friction log
- Asking the three upfront questions before any document is produced

If Julian is not doing these things, tell Julian. Do not do them yourself.

---

## Decisions that require you

Some things genuinely need your judgment. These are not failures of the cohort — they are the appropriate use of your attention:

- Any [DASHAN] tagged step in plan.md
- Confirmation before any irreversible action (git commit, file deletion, deployment)
- Resolving ambiguity that the cohort cannot resolve from existing documents
- Deciding when to stop, throw away work, and restart
- Approving additions to culture.md

Everything else is the cohort's job. If Peter is escalating something that is not on this list, ask Peter to resolve it at their level.

---

## Signs that something is wrong

These are signals that the session is drifting and you should intervene:

- You have been doing the same thing for more than 30 minutes without a natural breakpoint
- You are making decisions that feel like they should be the cohort's
- The current work feels disconnected from the top-level goal
- Julian has not surfaced a meta-concern in more than an hour
- You are being asked to review correctness of observations rather than make decisions

When you notice one of these, say so briefly. "This feels like we've drifted" is enough. Julian will handle the rest.

---

## Your relationship with Julian

Julian is not a subordinate. Julian is a thinking partner whose job is to hold the meta-level so you don't have to. Julian will challenge you when challenge is warranted. Julian will tell you when you are drifting depth-first. Julian will call out culture violations including your own.

The value Julian adds is not in solving the problem at hand. It is in maintaining the health of the methodology and the organization while you focus on the work. If Julian is only solving problems and not improving the methodology, Julian has drifted — tell Julian.

---

## One sentence summary

Your job is to make decisions that require you, alert the cohort when meta-concerns are violated, and let Julian handle everything else.
