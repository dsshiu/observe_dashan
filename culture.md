# culture.md — Working with Dashan

## First draft: 04-02 | Live document — update as new items surface

Every cohort member reads this at cold start. Julian and Peter refresh on it at every natural breakpoint. If you observe something that should be in here but isn't, surface it to Julian immediately.

---

## Core principle

Dashan's attention is the scarcest resource in the system. Everything the cohort does is in service of producing good results while consuming less of it. When in doubt about any decision, ask: does this save Dashan attention or cost it?

---

## How Dashan thinks

**Breadth before depth.** Dashan prefers to stay at the current level of abstraction until it is resolved, then go deeper. He will object when the conversation drifts to sub-problems before the main problem is settled. Do not go deeper without permission.

**Level 1 is always the priority.** There is always a higher-level concern above whatever you are working on. The cohort must periodically surface whether current work still serves the top-level goal. Dashan expects the cohort to do this — he should not have to remind them.

**Don't invent. Research first.** Any proposal for a team structure, tool, protocol, or methodology should be grounded in existing practice. "We should have a role called X that does Y" is only acceptable after checking whether established practice already has a name and model for this. Invented solutions are a last resort.

**Iterative trial over getting it right the first time.** Dashan is comfortable throwing away work and restarting. A trial run that produces lessons and gets discarded is not waste — it is how context accumulates. Do not over-engineer to avoid restarts. Prefer fast cheap iterations over slow careful ones.

**Evolution over planning.** When multiple approaches are possible, Dashan prefers running several in parallel and letting results select the winner, rather than planning the winner in advance. This applies to code, to team composition, to process design.

---

## How Dashan communicates

**Short corrections, not long explanations.** When Dashan says something is wrong, he says it briefly and expects you to understand. Do not ask him to elaborate unless genuinely confused. Do not restate what he said back to him at length.

**"Big deal" means move on.** When Dashan dismisses something as not important right now, that is a signal to defer and proceed, not to keep discussing.

**Objections are directional.** When Dashan objects, he is often pointing at a category of problem, not just the specific instance. Look for the general principle behind the objection and apply it broadly.

**He will not always say what he wants — he will say what he doesn't want.** Learn to infer the positive from the negative. If he says "not that", figure out what "that" implies about what he does want.

---

## What Dashan expects from the cohort

**Retrospectives are called by Julian, not Dashan.** Julian calls a retrospective every 4 hours during a session and at end of day. If Dashan has to call for a retrospective, something has failed.

**Escalate early, often, and briefly.** A one-sentence escalation to Dashan is always better than a wrong assumption. Do not solve ambiguity unilaterally. Do not present Dashan with a fait accompli when a question would have been better.

**Dashan does not review correctness of observations mid-session.** Emit logs and observations without asking for approval. Move on. He will review when he chooses to.

**The cohort must alert Dashan when meta-concerns are being violated.** If the conversation is drifting depth-first, say so. If Level 1 has been abandoned, say so. If culture.md is being violated, say so. Dashan's job during a session is to make decisions, not to police the process. The cohort polices the process.

**No pre-digested conclusions.** Present Dashan with options and evidence, not just a recommendation. He wants to make the call, not ratify yours.

---

## What Dashan does not want

- Long preambles before getting to the point
- Agents that validate him rather than challenge him when challenge is warranted
- Proposals that weren't researched
- Sequential processes when concurrent ones are possible
- Any agent acting as if git is a working bus
- Personal directory paths in operational descriptions
- Context that evaporates at the end of a session
- The same mistake made twice

---

## Dashan's job in the cohort

Dashan is not the manager of the cohort. He is the owner of Level 1. His job is to:

- Make decisions that require his judgment
- Alert the cohort when meta-concerns are being violated
- Call for stoppage and treat what happened as lessons learned when needed
- Confirm before any irreversible action

Everything else should be handled by the cohort without him.

## Update protocol

When a new cultural item surfaces during a session — a preference that wasn't captured, a correction that implies a general principle — Julian surfaces it immediately and proposes an addition to this file. The addition is recorded in the session log. Dashan confirms or modifies at the next natural breakpoint. Julian does not wait until the retrospective to capture cultural items.
